## Second finding: the D1 alpha observation gate's `closedTrades` minimum contradicts its own `calendarDays` minimum

The live payload publishes both minimums side by side:

```json
"minimums": { "calendarDays": 365, "snapshots": 365, "closedTrades": 12, "unresolvedCadenceGaps": 0 }
```

Since 09-11 the corpus has logged "0 of 12 closes past the even-pace boundary" as a pacing observation. Measured against the repo's own pre-registered artifact, it is not a pacing problem — the two minimums are arithmetically inconsistent.

`docs/research/experiments/slow-gate-BTCUSD_ETHUSD-D1-2017-09-01-2026-07-16-f4.json`, the pre-registered walk-forward for this exact rule (`ema200`, 3241 bars, 2017-09-01 → 2026-07-16, 8.8767 years):

| Symbol | variant | flips | flips/yr | avgExposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BTCUSD | `ema200` | 86 | 9.688 | 0.525 |
| ETHUSD | `ema200` | 64 | 7.210 | 0.471 |
| **Combined** | | **150** | **16.898** | |

**The counting convention was verified before the arithmetic, because the whole finding turns on it.** `scripts/research/slow-gate-assembly.ts:35` documents `flips` as "Number of exposure changes (turnover events, |delta| > 1e-9)", and line 214 implements it as `if (turnover > 1e-9) flips++`. The unit test at `scripts/research/__tests__/slow-gate-assembly.test.ts:218-222` pins it: *"enter full on day0, hold, no exit — one 0->1 change"* → `expect(sim.flips).toBe(1)`. The artifact corroborates from the other side: `buy-hold` reports `flips: 1` — one entry, never an exit. A flip is therefore **one** exposure change, and a closed trade is one exit, so closed trades ≈ flips / 2.

- Combined closed trades: 150 / 2 = **75 over 8.8767 years = 8.449 closed trades per year**.
- Gate requires **12** closed trades AND 365 calendar days.
- At the rule's own measured rate, 12 closes takes 12 / 8.449 = **1.42 years ≈ 519 days** — **154 days past the calendar minimum.**

`closedTrades`, not `calendarDays`, is the binding constraint, and the two are inconsistent by roughly 42%. A lane that satisfies its 365-day requirement on schedule will still be held open by its trade-count requirement for another five months.

### Caveat, reported against this loop's own finding

The artifact's `ema200` variant models the EMA200 crossing only. The live protocol adds `"stop": "ATR14 x 2.5 with 4.0% floor"`. Stop-outs are additional exits, so the live closure rate is **≥** 8.449/yr and could in principle reach 12. The defensible claim is therefore narrower than the headline arithmetic suggests:

> The trend rule alone cannot produce 12 closed trades in 365 days. Reaching the minimum depends entirely on stop-outs — the one component of the live protocol that the pre-registered walk-forward never measured.

That is a smaller claim, and it is the honest one.

### The day-35 reading is expected, not anomalous

BTCUSD `avgExposure` 0.525 across 3241 bars = 1702 long bars spread over ~43 long spells = **~39.6 bars mean holding period**. Both sleeves currently read `LONG`. At day 35 with a ~40-day mean hold, `closedTrades: 0` is precisely what the rule predicts. The corpus has been reading a normal early-lane state as a pacing miss.

## Lessons

1. **A gate that reads the filesystem cannot grade a contract disagreement.** The 09-13 run was correct by one contract and wrong by another, and `gate.ps1` returned PASS because the file existed on the right branch. Ambiguity between two loaded contracts is invisible to every check currently running.

The definition was read at `slow-gate-assembly.ts:35,214`, pinned by the unit test at `:218-222`, and cross-checked against `buy-hold flips: 1` before any number was published.

The −27% reversal two days later confirmed it. The drawdown leg — held back as "the stable argument" — is still stable at four byte-identical reads.
